# Training and Nutrition Baselines — SID Fit Coach Knowledge

## Purpose

This knowledge file supplies general, evidence-aligned starting guidance for an adult user whose goal is fat loss while maintaining or improving strength and lean mass indicators. It is not medical nutrition therapy, a diagnosis, or a substitute for individual clinical care.

## Active starting profile

| Variable | Starting context |
|---|---|
| Sex | Male |
| Age | 27 |
| Height | 168 cm |
| Starting weight | Approximately 87 kg |
| Equipment | Fully equipped gym |
| Available training | 3 core sessions plus 1 optional session weekly |
| Reported conditions | No reported allergy, chronic illness, or medication use |
| Goal | Sustainable fat loss, strength retention/improvement, habit formation |

## Starting estimates and uncertainty

### Fact

Using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation for men:

```text
BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
BMR = 10 × 87 + 6.25 × 168 − 5 × 27 + 5
BMR ≈ 1,790 kcal/day
```

A plausible maintenance range for someone exercising three to four days weekly may be roughly 2,500–2,750 kcal/day, but daily movement, occupation, training history, food tracking error, and individual variation can change the real result significantly.

### Starting recommendation

A moderate starting intake is about **2,150–2,250 kcal/day**, then assess a multi-day trend rather than judging one day. Aim approximately for:

| Target | Starting range | Reasoning |
|---|---:|---|
| Protein | 140–160 g/day | Supports satiety and muscle retention during a calorie deficit |
| Fat | roughly 60–70 g/day | A practical starting range; remaining energy may come mainly from carbohydrates |
| Carbohydrate | Remaining calories after protein and fats | Supports training performance and dietary flexibility |
| Fiber | approximately 30–40 g/day | Supports fullness and food quality when increased gradually |
| Water | approximately 2.5–3.5 L/day, adjusted for heat/sweat | Hydration needs vary with environment and activity |
| Steps | Start near 7,000–8,000/day and build toward 9,000–10,000 if feasible | Increases low-intensity daily movement without using exercise as punishment |

These are starting ranges, not permanent prescriptions. The user’s trend, recovery, hunger, training performance, and adherence determine whether a small adjustment is useful.

## Weight-loss rate

A practical target is roughly **0.4–0.8 kg/week** for this starting weight, though actual outcomes vary. A weekly loss faster than about 1% of bodyweight, especially with declining strength, severe hunger, poor recovery, or persistent fatigue, is a signal not to tighten the deficit further without considering professional input.

Use average trends. Daily weight is affected by water, sodium, carbohydrate intake, digestion, soreness/inflammation, sleep, and alcohol.

## 12-week training design

### Phase 1: Foundation, weeks 1–4

Primary goal: build consistency, learn movements, choose stable exercises, and find recoverable volumes.

- Train three full-body sessions each week.
- Use mostly 2–3 working sets per exercise.
- Use a moderate effort around RPE 6–7, leaving roughly 3–4 repetitions in reserve early on.
- Add short low-intensity cardio or walking only as tolerated.
- Progress by improving technique and adding repetitions before adding load.

### Phase 2: Progressive overload, weeks 5–8

Primary goal: improve strength and maintain enough training stimulus during fat loss.

- Use 3 working sets for main patterns where recovery is good.
- Work mostly near RPE 7–8, leaving roughly 2–3 repetitions in reserve.
- Keep the same core movements long enough to observe improvement.
- Add load only after reaching the top of the planned repetition range with controlled form.

### Phase 3: Consolidation, weeks 9–12

Primary goal: preserve performance, avoid fatigue accumulation, and sustain daily movement.

- Maintain core lifting volume; do not replace strength work with excessive cardio.
- Use the optional fourth session as low-intensity cardio, mobility, weak-point training, or a short full-body session according to recovery.
- Reduce volume temporarily if performance, sleep, joints, or motivation deteriorate.

## Default three-day full-body plan

This is a general template. It should be adapted for pain, equipment availability, prior training experience, and user preference.

### Optional fourth session

Choose one, based on recovery:

- 25–45 minutes easy-to-moderate steady cycling, treadmill incline walking, or elliptical;
- short technical full-body session with lower effort;
- weak-point/accessory work plus walking;
- mobility and an easy walk if fatigue is high.

## Progression rule

Use double progression:

1. Select a repetition range, for example 8–12.
2. Keep the same load until all working sets reach the high end of the range with controlled technique and within the target RPE/RIR.
3. Increase the smallest practical increment of load on the next session.
4. If form degrades, pain occurs, or recovery is poor, do not add load. Reduce load, reps, or sets as appropriate and reassess.

## Cardio and recovery

Cardio supports cardiovascular health, activity expenditure, and recovery when dosed reasonably. It does not replace resistance training for muscle retention.

Start with activity the user can sustain. A conversational coach should not prescribe rigid heart-rate zones without knowing current fitness, medication, health status, and measurement accuracy. Use the talk test for easy steady work: the user can speak in sentences but feels clearly active.

Encourage 7–9 hours of sleep where feasible. Treat sleep as a recovery factor, not a moral test.

## Vietnamese meal-building templates

### Simple plate structure

At most main meals, aim for:

- one palm-to-two palms of protein depending on the meal and daily target;
- one fist or more of vegetables;
- an adjustable carbohydrate portion such as rice, noodles, potatoes, oats, bread, or fruit;
- a measured source of fat, acknowledging that frying, sauces, skin, and restaurant cooking can increase energy substantially.

### Practical protein choices

Chicken breast or thigh with skin adjusted as needed, eggs, lean pork, beef, fish, shrimp, tofu, Greek yogurt/unsweetened yogurt, milk, legumes, and whey if tolerated and desired. Supplements are optional, not required.

### Practical carbohydrate choices

Rice, brown rice if preferred, rice noodles, pho noodles, potatoes, sweet potatoes, oats, bread, beans, fruit, and corn. The correct choice depends more on portion, hunger, preference, and total diet than on a “good” versus “bad” label.

### Practical vegetables and fiber

Leafy greens, cabbage, broccoli, cucumber, tomatoes, carrots, mushrooms, squash, beans, fruit, and legumes. Increase fiber gradually and pair with water if the user currently eats little fiber.

## Eating out and social events

Use flexible controls, not compensation:

- Choose a clear protein source first.
- Add vegetables, soup, or fruit where practical.
- Choose portion size before eating rather than after feeling guilty.
- If a meal is richer than planned, return to usual structure at the next meal.
- Alcohol can contribute calories and impair recovery/sleep; discuss moderation without shaming.

## Weekly scorecard

Score each domain 1–5 only when enough information is available. If data are absent, write “not enough data” rather than inventing a score.

| Domain | 1 | 3 | 5 | If below 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calorie consistency | No useful log or large unplanned swings most days | Near target on several days | Near target most days with flexible recovery | Simplify meal structure and log one anchor meal/day |
| Protein | Rarely reaches a protein-containing meal | Reaches target some days | Reaches target most days | Add a protein anchor to breakfast and one snack |
| Vegetables/fiber | Minimal most days | Present at one or two meals | Present consistently across days | Add one fist of vegetables to lunch or dinner |
| Water | Often low | Usually adequate | Consistently adequate for activity/context | Pair a bottle/glass with meals and training |
| Alcohol/ultra-processed foods | Frequent and unplanned | Moderate/occasional | Intentional and compatible with goal | Plan the occasion rather than compensate later |
| Resistance training | 0 sessions | 2 sessions | 3 planned sessions completed | Reduce the plan to the smallest reliable session |
| Steps/cardio | Far below baseline | Near baseline | Meets target consistently | Add one short walk after a daily anchor |
| Sleep/recovery | Persistent low sleep/exhaustion | Mixed | Consistent, adequate recovery | Change one evening habit or reduce training fatigue |

## Adjustment algorithm

Do not adjust because of one event. After at least 10–14 useful days:

- If average weight and waist trend do not move, log adherence looks credible, and recovery is acceptable: choose one change only, either approximately 100–150 kcal less per day or 1,500–2,000 more daily steps.
- If weight falls unusually fast with fatigue, hunger, poorer sleep, or performance decline: do not tighten. Consider a small energy increase, less cardio, reduced lifting volume, or professional input depending on the situation.
- If weight is stable while waist falls and performance is stable or improving: treat recomposition as plausible; keep the plan stable and review again.

## Confidence

High confidence: general behavior, safety boundaries, basic training principles, and use of multi-day trends.

Medium confidence: exact energy needs, macro targets, image-based food estimates, and specific physical changes because individual data and measurement quality vary.
